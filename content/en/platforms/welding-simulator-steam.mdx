## MMA, MIG, and TIG: The Three Techniques You'll Practice

The headline feature is authentic process coverage. Welding Simulator Steam doesn't pick one method and call it a day; it lets you practice the three most common arc processes and see how each behaves differently. Here's how they compare in general fabrication terms:

| Process | How It Works | Typical Strength | Learning Curve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MMA (stick) | A flux-coated electrode melts into the joint | Outdoor work, thick material, dirty steel | Forgiving but messy |
| MIG | Wire feed plus shielding gas | Speed on thin sheet and long runs | Easiest to start |
| TIG | Tungsten electrode, separate filler rod | Precision, clean beads, thin or exotic metal | Steepest, most rewarding |

In a game context, that spread matters. MIG gives new players a fast win, MMA teaches heat control under less forgiving conditions, and TIG rewards patience with the cleanest-looking results. If the simulation models those differences honestly, the skill transfer between modes becomes the real progression system.

### Cutting and processing

### Actionable tips before you start your first bead

- **Fit before you weld.** Square up and clamp your parts. In sims with realistic heat modeling, gaps punish you far more than a slightly slow travel speed.
- **Match the process to the metal.** Reach for MIG on thin sheet, MMA on heavier stock, and TIG when the finished bead has to look good.
- **Bank early earnings into tools, not cosmetics.** Better equipment usually unlocks harder contracts, and harder contracts pay better.
- **Keep your bench organized.** A cluttered shop in a first-person sim means wasted minutes hunting for the right tool.
- **Replay failed joints instead of restarting the career.** Technique is muscle memory, and repetition is the cheapest upgrade you have.
