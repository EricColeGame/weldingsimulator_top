## Welding Techniques You Will Practice: MMA, MIG, and TIG

The core promise of the Welding Simulator game Steam store page is technique, not just construction. The listing names three processes, and each one behaves very differently in the real world. Understanding the difference now will help you pick a starting point when the game arrives.

| Technique | Real-World Behavior | Likely In-Game Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MMA (stick / manual metal arc) | Flux-coated electrode, slag cleanup, forgiving outdoors and on windy sites | Striking and holding an arc, thicker steel, cleanup |
| MIG (metal inert gas) | Continuously fed wire, fast deposition, easy on thin sheet | Wire feed speed, travel speed, long consistent beads |
| TIG (tungsten inert gas) | Tungsten electrode with hand-fed filler, cleanest and most precise, slowest | Heat control, precision, thin-wall tubing |

A practical way to think about it: MMA teaches you arc discipline, MIG teaches you speed and consistency, and TIG teaches you patience. Simulators tend to reward the same habits real welders develop — steady travel speed, correct angle, and heat management — so treat each process as its own skill tree rather than a reskin of the last one.

### Cutting, Prep, and Fit-Up

The listing also highlights cutting and processing metal before you join it. In practice, fit-up decides how a weld looks and holds:

- Measure twice and mark clearly before any cut.
- Keep gaps tight and consistent along the joint.
- Clean edges before joining — contamination ruins bead quality.
- Tack weld first, then check square before running a full pass.

These habits translate directly from workshop to screen, and they are the difference between a project that looks engineered and one that looks improvised.
