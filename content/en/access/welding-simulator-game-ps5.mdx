## MMA, MIG, and TIG: The Skills at the Center of the Game

The sim's selling point is that it teaches three real welding disciplines rather than one generic "spark" mechanic. Here is how they differ, and what each one typically demands:

| Technique | Real-World Basics | What Players Should Expect |
|---|---|---|
| MMA (stick) | Flux-coated electrode creates the arc and shielding | Slower, forgiving, good for thicker metal |
| MIG | Wire feed with shielding gas for fast, clean beads | Speed-focused, smooth seams, beginner-friendly |
| TIG | Tungsten electrode plus a separate filler rod | Precise, delicate, hardest to master |

Each method rewards different habits — travel speed, heat control, and angle — which is why the game frames itself as a safe place to experiment. Beginners can burn through virtual metal without wasting stock, and experienced hobbyists can compare techniques side by side. For anyone who has priced out a real TIG rig, that alone has appeal, which is part of why the **Welding Simulator game PS5** question keeps coming up in hobbyist forums.
