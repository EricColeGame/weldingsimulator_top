## MMA vs. MIG vs. TIG: Which Technique Fits Your Playstyle

The three processes are the heart of the game, and in real shops they behave very differently. If you're deciding where to spend your practice time first, this comparison is the most practical table in the article.

| Technique | Real-World Character | Likely In-Game Role | Learning Curve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MMA (stick)** | Consumable electrode, forgiving on dirty or rusty metal, works outdoors, better on thicker stock | Structural joints, table legs, frame repairs, general fabrication | Easiest entry point |
| **MIG (wire feed)** | Continuous wire electrode, fast deposition, simple to run, excellent on sheet and tube | Bike frames, go-kart chassis, long beads on thin-to-medium metal | Moderate — easy to start, harder to master |
| **TIG (tungsten)** | Non-consumable tungsten electrode with separate filler, slow, precise, beautiful beads | Visible seams, thin-walled parts, detail work, finish-quality joints | Steepest — rewards patience |

A sensible progression is MMA first, MIG second, TIG last. Stick welding punishes sloppy technique less, so it gives you room to learn arc length and travel speed without wrecking every piece. MIG then teaches consistency across long runs, which is exactly what a bike frame or go-kart chassis demands. TIG is where precision becomes the whole point — and where a simulator arguably earns its keep most, since TIG mistakes are expensive in real life.

Actionable tip: whatever process the game defaults you to, spend your first session deliberately producing bad welds. Too fast, too slow, too much current, too little. Learning to recognize a cold lap or a blown-through edge by sight and sound is a transferable skill, and it's free in a simulation.

The announced loop is straightforward: take jobs, get paid, buy better equipment, take bigger jobs. What makes or breaks that loop is whether the project list escalates in a way that forces you to actually improve rather than simply grind.

| Project Tier | Example Builds | Skills Exercised | Progression Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Metal tables, simple frames | Straight beads, square cuts, basic fit-up | Seed money and tool familiarity |
| Mid | Bike frames, custom fixtures | Thin-wall tubing, heat control, alignment | Better gear, larger workspace |
| Advanced | Go-karts, complex structures | Multi-part assembly, strength-critical joints | Higher payouts, expanded project menu |
| Ongoing | Repairs and custom jobs | Diagnosis, cutting, rework | Flexible income between big builds |

Workshop customization is the second half of that loop. Upgrading gear and expanding space isn't cosmetic — in most trade simulators, better tools translate directly into cleaner results and faster jobs. If the system is well designed, you'll feel the difference between a cheap machine and a good one in the bead itself, not just in a stat screen.

Three habits to bring into career mode:

1. **Finish clean before you finish fast.** Rework costs more than patience in every fabrication sim ever made.
2. **Cut once, measure twice.** The listing specifically calls out cutting and processing, so expect fit-up errors to be a real failure state.
3. **Reinvest early.** Upgrading the machine you use most usually beats spreading money across every tool.

Because there are no user reviews yet, we can't confirm how deep the upgrade tree goes or how many project types exist beyond the ones named. Community reports will be the place to watch once the game launches.

## FAQ

**Is Welding Simulator the best game for learning real welding?**
No simulation replaces hands-on training with a certified instructor, and this one is explicitly framed as a safe, accessible way to explore the craft. That said, learning to read a puddle, control travel speed, and understand the difference between MMA, MIG, and TIG has genuine value as preparation before you ever strike a real arc.

**When does Welding Simulator come out?**
The release date is currently listed as "to be announced" on Steam. You can add it to your wishlist on the store page to be notified when a date is confirmed. No price has been announced either.

**Which welding technique should I start with in Welding Simulator?**
Start with MMA (stick). It's the most forgiving process on thicker, less-than-clean metal, so it gives you room to build fundamentals. Move to MIG for speed and long beads on tubing, then tackle TIG once precision matters more than speed.

**Can my PC run Welding Simulator?**
If you have a 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 machine with 8 GB of RAM and something in the range of a GTX 750 or RX Vega 8, you meet the minimum. For smoother performance, aim for 16 GB of RAM and a GTX 1660-class card. The 2 GB install size means storage won't be a concern.
