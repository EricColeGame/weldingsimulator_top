## Technique Depth: MMA, MIG, and TIG in Welding Simulator

DM Soft built the game around three processes that cover the majority of real shop work. Each one asks you to juggle different variables, and each rewards a different kind of patience. That structure is what separates the title from arcade-style welding games.

| Process | What it is | Common real-world use | What you manage in-game |
|---|---|---|---|
| MMA (stick) | A flux-coated consumable electrode strikes the arc and generates its own shielding | Repairs, outdoor work, thick steel | Amperage, arc length, travel speed, slag cleanup |
| MIG (wire feed) | Continuous wire electrode fed through a gun with shielding gas | Thin sheet, production work, fast fabrication | Wire feed speed, voltage, gas flow, torch angle |
| TIG (tungsten) | Non-consumable tungsten electrode paired with a separate filler rod | Stainless, aluminum, thin precision joints | Heat input, filler feed, gas coverage, hand steadiness |

A few habits that pay off regardless of which process you favor:

- **Learn one process at a time.** Timing from stick welding does not transfer cleanly to TIG, and mixing them early builds bad muscle memory.
- **Watch the puddle, not the arc.** The bright arc is the distraction; the molten pool tells you whether you are traveling too fast or running too hot.
- **Lock in travel speed before adding filler.** Consistent motion produces consistent beads, and consistency is what the job scoring rewards.
- **Treat cleanup as part of the job.** Chip slag and inspect every pass — in a simulation, a hidden defect tends to surface at the worst possible moment.
- **Practice cutting before joining.** Square, accurate cuts make every downstream weld easier, and the game models cutting as a real step.
